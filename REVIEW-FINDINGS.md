| Receptor handed to `--receptor` | Ligand-Receptor energy |

The `--score_only` decomposition is the diagnosis, and it takes one minute to run.

#### Finding 1: The raw-PDB receptor gives Vina an identically zero hydrogen-bond term; the cause is atom typing, not charges, and the effect is total rather than a -1.4 to -1.9 offset

- **Kind:** error
- **Severity:** critical
- **Target:** both

**Evidence:** Measured today with the bundled vina.exe 1.2.7, glucose at its crystal pose (validation/glc_ligand.pdbqt carries the GLC 601 coordinates), box 21.52/-7.70/23.55, 30^3, --score_only. prepared/receptor.pdbqt: Ligand-Receptor = -5.732 kcal/mol. receptor_clean.pdb: -0.067 kcal/mol. Re-run with --weight_hydrogen 0: prepared falls to -0.067, raw stays -0.067 (unchanged, i.e. its H-bond term is exactly zero). A synthetic PDBQT with identical coordinates typed C/N/O/S reproduces -0.067 exactly, proving the parse is fine and the defect is typing; retyping only O->OA and S->SA gives -3.068. Type census: prepared/receptor.pdbqt = 923 OA, 14 NA, 1061 HD; receptor_clean.pdb cols 77-78 = 3095 C, 804 N, 923 O, 13 S, zero H, zero HETATM.

**Why it matters:** For the co-crystal ligand the H-bond term is 98.8 percent of the interaction energy (-5.665 of -5.732). The 10-compound screen therefore ranked candidates with a receptor that had zero H-bond donors AND zero acceptors, i.e. on steric burial alone, which is why the polar glycosides and acarbose gained most and the triterpenes least. Two consequences for today's write-up. (1) The stated mechanism is wrong: Vina has no electrostatic term, so "Gasteiger charges" (validation.json:5, SOURCES.md:25-28, docking_backend.py:106 and :201, REDOCK-PROTOCOL.md:177) is not the difference, and missing polar hydrogens explain only about 46 percent of it; the other 54 percent is the O->OA / N->NA acceptor mis-typing, which adding hydrogens would not fix. (2) The Delta column in REDOCK-PROTOCOL.md:54-61 invites reading the published numbers as a fixed offset from the truth. They are not; they are the optimum of a different objective function, so no correction restores the published ranking.

**What to do:** Restate the mechanism as atom typing (O vs OA, N vs NA, absent HD) and delete "Gasteiger charges" as the explanation everywhere it appears. Present the prepared-receptor numbers as a re-run on a different scoring input, not as a shift on a shared scale. Add the score_only decomposition above to the methods: it is one command and it settles the point far more convincingly than the RMSD table. Also record that Vina accepts a .pdb receptor silently: I confirmed it types a PDB-format calcium (element CA in cols 77-78) as aromatic carbon and a zinc as nitrogen, while CL and MG hard-fail. The shipped 4GQR alpha-amylase library target (docking_backend.py:665-675, which keeps CA and CL via _KEEP_HET at :525) dies with "Atom type L is not a valid AutoDock type" when run through _strip_to_protein plus the raw path, so that target currently cannot dock at all.

#### Finding 2: "The validated receptor" outruns one sugar redock: the protonation model it rests on is undocumented, unreproducible, and uniform-by-default

- **Kind:** overreach
- **Severity:** major
- **Target:** todays_analysis

**Evidence:** REDOCK-PROTOCOL.md:67 ("On the validated receptor..."), :175-177 ("Fixed, and not negotiable" / "Meeko, polar H + Gasteiger charges") and :104-105, which pins receptor_H.pdb by SHA-256 AD102AF2ED78C79A. No script anywhere in the repo produces receptor_H.pdb; the only related file is a console-script shim, talanaidock/dist/TalanaiDock/python/mk_prepare_receptor.py, and Meeko's mk_prepare_receptor does not add hydrogens. Empirically the protonation is a blanket default: all 14 histidines carry the identical NE2-H tautomer (14 HE2, 0 HD1; the 14 NA atoms in the PDBQT are all ND1), and 0 of the ASP/GLU carboxylates are protonated, including the general acid Glu277 whose OE1/OE2 lie 4.8 and 4.9 A from the box centre.

**Why it matters:** A 0.52 A redock of a small rigid sugar validates pose recovery; it does not validate the donor/acceptor assignment, and per finding 1 that assignment is essentially the entire score in this pocket. The receptor now being promoted to canonical is the only one in the study whose preparation cannot be reproduced from the repository: a hash pins the bytes but is not a recipe, so a reviewer cannot regenerate it or check it. Uniform HIE across 14 histidines and a deprotonated general acid are defensible defaults but they are choices, and the margins the conclusion turns on (quercetin -8.889 versus acarbose mean -8.730, a 0.16 kcal/mol gap) are smaller than a single active-site tautomer flip is worth.

**What to do:** Record the exact protonation route (tool, version, pH, His tautomer policy) and regenerate receptor_H.pdb from a committed script so the "validated" receptor is as reproducible as the raw one. Check the histidines and Glu277 against their local H-bond networks, or run PROPKA, rather than accepting a blanket tautomer. Then dock at least the top three compounds against one alternative protonation variant and report the spread, so the ranking is shown not to be an artefact of the default. State plainly in the write-up that the control validates pose recovery only.

- **Kind:** error
- **Severity:** major

But acarbose has 44 heavy atoms, the maximum in the set, so on the prepared numbers it is last by construction (0.195) behind quercetin 0.404, kaempferol 0.392, betulinic 0.292, rutin 0.247, oleanolic 0.236, and the vault's 'Everything still beats Acarbose (0.151) on both measures' is close to arithmetically guaranteed rather than independent confirmation.

**Why it matters:** Vina is a triage tool and is defensible as one: 'ranked most favourably under identical settings' is a fair statement. 'Binds more tightly' is not, because the differences being asserted are smaller than the method's own standard error, and the reader has no way to know that from the page. The ligand-efficiency column makes it worse rather than better: Vina's reported score is already torsion-normalised, so LE applies a second, uncalibrated size penalty on top of a size-penalised quantity, and LE's well-documented intrinsic size dependence simply moves the winner from the largest molecule (rutin) to the smallest (quercetin/kaempferol). Presenting it as the correction implies the size confound has been dealt with when it has only been inverted, and it lends false independence to the acarbose comparison specifically, since acarbose is the biggest molecule in the set.

**What to do:** Reword to 'predicted a more favourable binding score than acarbose under identical settings' and state the scoring function's error magnitude in the same sentence, once, site-wide. Relabel the LE column as a second confounded view rather than the size correction, and amend R701's remedy text in rules.py to say that LE reverses the size bias rather than removing it. Where LE is quoted alongside the raw ranking (vault note, R702 output), drop the 'on both measures' framing for acarbose and note that it is the largest compound in the set. If an actual affinity ranking is wanted, it needs an orthogonal method (rescoring, MM-GBSA, or an enzyme assay), not a second column derived by dividing the same number.

**Refutation verdict:** Not refuted, medium confidence; one narrow limb survives, most of the framing does not. Surviving: LE's intrinsic size dependence really does invert rather than remove the confound here (rutin falls from rank 1 on raw score to rank 4 on LE, quercetin rises to rank 1), so treating "everything beats acarbose on both measures" as independent confirmation is wrong; that criticism attaches specifically to vault 2026-07-31.md:68. What fails: (1) the "binds more tightly" charge cherry-picks two unhedged instances (an aria-label and one sentence) against consistently hedged visible copy elsewhere ("predicted to," "in silico docking ... predicts"); the real defect is an accessibility-parity bug (screen-reader text unhedged where visible text is hedged), not a systemic overclaim. (2) The "lives inside the function's error bar" argument misapplies absolute calibration error (~2.85 kcal/mol, for predicting experimental affinity) to same-target relative ranking, where systematic error largely cancels; applied consistently it would invalidate all comparative docking. (3) "Vina's score is already torsion-normalised, so LE is a second size penalty" is technically wrong: Vina normalises by rotatable bonds, not heavy atoms, and the two decouple sharply in this set (betulinic: 33 heavy atoms, 4 torsions; rutin: 43 heavy atoms, 16 torsions). Correction: narrow the finding to the LE-independence point and the aria-label/visible-copy mismatch; drop the rest.
